The proposal · scope as in build plan rev 4

Five phases, in order, with the useful part first.

Phase 1 is a working tracker on its own. Everything after it removes manual work from something Phase 1 already does, so there is no phase you have to wait for before the system is worth using.

1

The tracker

Every shipment on one board. Three status views in your own words: where the cargo is, where the entry is, and who in your office has it now. The five-day clock from actual arrival, counting calendar days, with the bands. Problems with owners and dates. The attention list, ranked by severity then age. Your reports, with delays split into within and outside DCA's control. Your departments and permissions, set up as you answered. Your current sheet imported with its history.

2

Alerts

Viber alerts for what the attention list has already caught, so nobody has to be at the screen.

3

Customs status from the VASP

Sent / Lodged, Selected, Final, Paid / Debited fill themselves in on a schedule. InterCommerce first, then CDEC, then eKonek. Your password is never kept inside the system, every check is logged, and the connection shuts itself off if logins start failing.

4

Cargo status from the shipping line

ETA, actual arrival and container status from the line's own tracking. This is the feed that writes the date the whole five-day clock runs on. Where a date is entered by hand, the system records that it was.

5

The Trackify connection

One-way, read-only. Trackify reads; it can never write. One key per customer, off until you turn it on, every request logged. Your internal statuses, targets, performance, staff and finances never cross.

Before any of it · one gate

We read your live tracking sheet against the design and fix the design to fit your real data, not the other way round. Dates go on the phases only after that, and then we mean them.

What "working" means

Before Phase 1 starts we write down, in one page, the pass/fail standard for each phase. It works against that page, or we are not finished. Your staff use it every day. Not a demo.

Three catches, built in

A shipment with no recorded arrival is named on the attention list, never shows a reassuring zero, and gets its own line on the report, excluded from every average.

Still open · one line each, none of them changes the price

Arrived Monday 3 August: due end of Saturday the 8th, or Friday the 7th? · Approaching Target: badge at 5, list at 2, or both the same? · Roughly how many shipments a month, and how many rows in the current tracker?

Terms

In plain words. Numbered so we can point to them, not to make them longer.

What is included

Everything on sheet 3: the five phases of Clearway as described in build plan rev 4, the sheet review before Phase 1, the written pass/fail standard, import of your current tracker with its history, set-up of your departments and people, and hand-over to your staff. Where this document and rev 4 differ, this document wins.

Price and payment

₱100,000.00 excluding VAT for the whole build. VAT at 12% is added on the invoice. Half is due on acceptance and half when Phase 1 is running in DCA against the written standard. The price is fixed for the scope at item 01; changes to scope are agreed in writing first, with a price, before any work on them starts.

What DCA owns

A permanent, company-wide right to use Clearway, for as long as you like, for everyone in the company, with no per-seat charge and nothing that expires. Your data is yours: every shipment, client, document, reason and number stays with you, and you can export all of it at any time. The system is delivered running on infrastructure you control, with the keys in your hands.

What Andar keeps

Andar owns the software, its source code and its design, and reserves the right to resell, license and white-label Clearway, and anything derived from it, to other brokers and other businesses under their own name or ours. This is the basis on which the early-customer price is offered. It covers the machine only; item 05 covers what never leaves DCA.

What never leaves DCA

Your shipments, clients, staff, departments, documents, reasons, targets, performance figures and your tracking sheet are confidential and will not be shared with, or shown to, any other customer. The name DC Abay-abay Customs Brokerage and any reference to you as a customer are used only with your written say-so. The data-sharing agreement for Trackify, item 07, is separate and is signed before that connection exists.

What "finished" means

Before Phase 1 starts we write the pass/fail standard for each phase on one page and you approve it. A phase is finished when it meets that page and your staff are using it. If it does not meet the page, we fix it at no charge until it does.

What we need from you

The full export of your current tracker before the sheet review. The two one-line answers on the clock and the approaching-target split. A written go-ahead to check the VASP portals on your behalf, and a sub-account where the VASP allows one, before Phase 3. The short data-sharing agreement before Phase 5. Delays on your side move dates, not the price.

Third parties

Fees charged by others are not included and are paid by DCA directly: hosting and domain, Viber or messaging charges, VASP accounts, and anything on the Trackify side. We will tell you what each costs before it is incurred. Andar does not touch Trackify itself.

After delivery

Fixes to anything that does not meet the written standard are free. Keeping it running afterwards, with changes and the next thing on the list, is a separate monthly arrangement, offered but not required. Without it, Clearway keeps working as delivered.

Dates, validity and law

Dates are put on the phases after the sheet review, in writing, and then held to. This proposal is valid for 30 days from 4 September 2026. It is governed by the laws of the Republic of the Philippines; either side may end the work in writing at the end of any phase, paying for the phases delivered against the standard.
